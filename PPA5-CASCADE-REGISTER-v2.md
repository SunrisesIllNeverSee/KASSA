# Background

## The Distribution Problem

Existing software distribution platforms employ one of two models for market entry: unrestricted listing (anyone can list, quality is unknown until after purchase) or editorial gatekeeping (a human review team decides what is listed). Neither model provides a market-based, zero-risk demand validation mechanism that protects buyers while simultaneously filtering product quality without subjective editorial judgment.

No existing platform implements a demand-validated market entry mechanism where the market itself --- through the binary act of purchasing --- determines whether a product merits full distribution, with zero financial risk to buyers if the market rejects the product.

## The Pricing Problem

Existing software pricing is either static (fixed price) or dynamic in ways that do not reward early adopters. No existing system implements sequential wave pricing where early buyers receive a structural price advantage, each tier must be fully consumed before the next activates, the progression is deterministic and transparent, and the sell-through requirement creates a continuous validation signal.

Furthermore, no existing system anchors software pricing to a transparent, formula-derived estimate of the product's lifetime economic value. SaaS pricing is set by the seller with no buyer-visible methodology linking the price to the product's economic fundamentals. The invention introduces a lifetime value pricing formula that makes the relationship between seat price and estimated product value visible to every buyer.

## The Instrument Problem

Existing software purchases produce receipts, not instruments. No existing software distribution system issues serialized, transferable instruments with cryptographic lineage, dual-signature verification, emblem generation upon escrow release, configurable buyback obligations, or distribution allocations enabling founders to reserve seats for team, advisors, or strategic purposes.

Existing tokenized instrument systems (blockchain-deployed digital tickets, NFT-based access rights, smart-contract license management) conflate instrument provenance with distributed ledger deployment, requiring on-chain state, smart-contract execution, and public blockchain verification as architectural prerequisites. The present invention takes a fundamentally different approach: seat instruments are **platform-managed server-side entitlements** --- not blockchain-deployed tokens or smart contracts. Provenance is established through **append-only cryptographic hash chains maintained by the platform**, with verification provided through **digitally signed receipts and audit proofs** rather than public ledger lookups or smart-contract state queries. Transfer enforcement is **server-mediated** --- the platform enforces transfer policies through application logic rather than instrument-embedded smart-contract code. This architectural distinction is deliberate: it preserves the full cryptographic verifiability of instrument lineage (any party can verify the hash chain and signatures) while eliminating dependency on distributed consensus, gas fees, blockchain network availability, and the architectural constraints inherent in smart-contract-based instrument systems.

## The Negotiation Problem

No existing software distribution system implements structured buyer-initiated price negotiation where buyers submit offers, sellers accept/reject/counter, and the negotiation history is recorded in the instrument's cryptographic lineage.

## The Agent Problem

Existing AI agent platforms (agent frameworks, skill registries, multi-agent orchestration tools) enable agents to discover information, generate content, and execute tasks, but no existing system enables AI agents to participate as constitutionally governed actors in commercial marketplace transactions. Agents cannot browse a marketplace's structured API, carry referral attribution, stage purchase intents for human confirmation, or earn commission for their operators through discovery and referral --- all under a governance framework that records the agent's declared capabilities and constitutional constraints at the time of action.

The absence of agent-mediated commerce infrastructure means that as autonomous agents become primary discovery channels, commercial marketplaces have no mechanism for agent attribution, agent-staged transactions, or agent governance in commercial contexts.

## The Recursive Governance Problem

No existing commercial distribution system implements a recursive governance architecture wherein the governance tool used to configure marketplace participants is itself distributed as a product through that marketplace. This architectural recursion --- where the first product validates the system's mechanics and the system's mechanics distribute the first product --- has no prior art in commercial software distribution.

### Design-Around Rationale

The following architectural choices are informed by freedom-to-operate analysis and are integral to the Cascade Register's design:

1. **Server-side entitlements, not blockchain tokens.** Seat instruments are platform-managed database records with cryptographic integrity provided by append-only hash chains and dual signatures — not smart-contract tokens deployed to a blockchain. This avoids the instrument/policy/escrow/chronology claim patterns in US11907916B2 and the general tokenization patterns in US12154087B2, while preserving full cryptographic verifiability.

2. **Fixed-price sell-through as primary mechanism.** Wave pricing is deterministic and fixed by the founder. The optional Offer Negotiation Layer is a bilateral secondary overlay, not a proposal-based allocation system. This avoids the sequential offer/bid allocation patterns in US12148026B2.

3. **Server-mediated transfer enforcement.** Transfer policies are enforced by platform application logic, not by instrument-embedded smart-contract code. This avoids bridge-transfer patterns in US11341466B2.

4. **Signed-receipt verification, not public ledger lookups.** Verification is performed via platform-signed receipts and independently verifiable hash-chain audit proofs, not via public blockchain state queries. This avoids the public-ledger verification patterns in US10931457B2.

5. **Sell-through gates, not time-window breakpoints.** Wave transitions are triggered by complete sell-through (purchase count), not by time windows with retroactive discount/refund mechanics. This avoids the quantity-linked breakpoint patterns in US11861637B2.

# Definitions

The following definitions apply throughout this disclosure unless otherwise specified:

**Cascade Register.** The complete commercial distribution system comprising all eleven components disclosed herein.

**Wave Zero.** The demand-validated market entry mechanism requiring a fixed number of seat purchases at a platform-standard price under all-or-nothing escrow before a product's full cascade distribution opens. Wave Zero is a platform-wide constant.

**Wave.** A discrete pricing tier within a cascade, defined by seat quantity, unit price, and a sell-through requirement.

**Sell-Through Gate.** The structural enforcement mechanism requiring every seat in a wave to be purchased before the next wave activates. Cannot be overridden.

**All-or-Nothing Escrow.** The escrow mechanism governing Wave Zero in which buyer funds are held and not released until every seat is purchased. Full refund if never completed.

**Per-Seat Escrow.** The escrow mechanism governing Waves 1+ in which each purchase is independently escrowed for a holding period before fund release.

**Cascade.** The complete sequence of waves through which a product is distributed. Defined by type, seat quantities, pricing, multiplier, and wave count.

**Cascade Type.** SaaS Cascade (larger seat quantities, broad distribution) or Enterprise Cascade (smaller seat quantities, exclusive positioning).

**Price Multiplier.** The factor $\mu$ by which seat price increases at each wave transition. Default: $\mu = 1.6$.

**Estimated Lifetime Value (eLTV).** A formula-derived estimate of the total economic value a buyer receives from a product over its expected lifespan, computed as the product of the monthly price and a conservative lifespan estimate in months: $\text{eLTV} = P_{\text{monthly}} \times L_{\text{months}}$.

**LTV Multiplier.** The ratio of the current wave price to the product's eLTV, expressed as $M_{\text{LTV}} = P_w / \text{eLTV}$. Values below 1.0 indicate the seat is priced below estimated lifetime value; values above 1.0 indicate a premium.

**Seat Instrument.** A serialized, transferable commercial instrument representing perpetual access rights to a software product. Carries serial number, wave of origin, price paid, escrow status, lineage, dual-signature verification, and emblem.

**Serial Number.** Unique identifier formatted as KS-YYYY-NNNNN.

**Seat Lineage.** Cryptographic hash chain recording every event in an instrument's history from mint through transfers.

**Emblem.** A verifiable visual artifact generated upon escrow release, cryptographically bound to the seat instrument and serving as proof of ownership and wave provenance.

**Distribution Allocation.** Seats reserved by the founder from any wave for team members, advisors, investors, or strategic partners. Allocated seats carry the same serial and lineage but are marked as allocated rather than purchased.

**Buyback Clause.** A founder-configured obligation defining the terms under which the founder will repurchase a seat instrument if the underlying product ceases operation. Options include pro-rata buyback (refund proportional to remaining estimated lifespan) and full buyback.

**Offer.** A buyer-initiated price submission below the current wave price for a seat in an active post-Wave-Zero wave.

**Marketplace Board.** The full-inventory discovery surface with curated shelves and sector-tabbed browsing.

**Floating Moat Calculator.** A buyer-facing tool displaying, for each listing, the LTV multiplier, monthly equivalent cost, estimated value position, and comparison to estimated lifetime value, enabling buyers to evaluate instrument pricing against product economics.

**Product Score.** A composite quality metric computed from sell-through velocity, demand trend, value positioning, and completion rate.

**Founder Score.** A separate reputation metric computed from the founder's history across all listings, including Wave Zero completion rate, cascade performance, buyback honor rate, and verification status.

**Trend Indicator.** A directional signal (Rising, Steady, Cooling) derived from rolling-window analysis.

**Agent.** An autonomous software system capable of multi-step tasks and actions without constant human supervision.

**Operator.** The human or legal entity that deploys, configures, and bears legal responsibility for an agent's actions.

**Principal.** The human on whose behalf an agent acts in a specific transaction. The principal owns the seat.

**Transaction Firewall.** The architectural boundary ensuring that agents can discover, recommend, and stage purchases, but only a human can confirm and execute a financial transaction.

**Intent.** A structured declaration by an agent that its principal wishes to purchase a seat. An intent is not a transaction; it requires human confirmation.

**Constitutional Frame.** The set of encoded governance constraints under which an agent operates, configured via the governance console. Constraints are verifiable, auditable, and hash-recorded.

**Referral Attribution.** The mechanism by which an agent's role in discovering or surfacing a listing is recorded and compensated. Attribution is tied to the operator.

**Agent Registry.** The enrollment system identifying every agent interacting with the marketplace beyond public browsing, recording declared capabilities and constitutional constraints.

**Merchant of Record (MoR).** The legal entity that serves as the seller of record for all marketplace transactions, handling payment processing, tax collection, regulatory compliance, and disbursement to founders.

**Recursive Governance Architecture.** The structural property wherein the governance console used to configure agent participation in the marketplace is itself the first product distributed through that marketplace, creating a self-referential loop where the product governs the environment in which it is sold.

# Detailed Description of the Preferred Embodiments

## Component 1: Wave Zero Proving Ground

**Core idea.** Every product entering the Cascade Register must clear an identical demand validation gate before accessing full distribution. Wave Zero is a live product purchase at a standardized price under conditions that protect buyers from product failure while requiring genuine market demand before the founder receives any revenue.

**Structural constants.** Wave Zero parameters are platform-wide constants, identical for every product:

- Seat count: A fixed number of seats (the platform constant $N_0$).
- Price per seat: A fixed platform-standard price $P_0$.
- Escrow type: All-or-nothing.
- Negotiation: Disabled. No offers permitted during Wave Zero.
- Time limit: None. Wave Zero remains open until it fills or the founder withdraws.

**All-or-nothing escrow mechanics.** Buyer funds are held in platform escrow upon purchase. No funds are released to the founder until all $N_0$ seats are purchased. The escrow state machine has three terminal states:

```text
HOLDING ——→ RELEASED (all N_0 seats filled → funds to founder)
HOLDING ——→ REFUNDED (founder withdraws listing → funds to all buyers)
HOLDING ——→ REFUNDED (platform removes listing for policy violation → funds to all buyers)
```

**What Wave Zero proves.** If $N_0$ independent buyers commit $P_0$ each, the product has demonstrated minimum viable demand --- a stronger signal than page views (passive), signups (free), reviews (gameable), or editorial selection (subjective).

**Buyer experience.** A Wave Zero buyer pays $P_0$ and receives: immediate product access (real purchase, not deposit); a serialized seat instrument; and the structural guarantee of full refund if Wave Zero never completes.

## Component 2: Wave Cascade Engine

**Core idea.** After Wave Zero clears, the founder's custom cascade activates with deterministic pricing progression.

**Cascade configuration.** Founders configure:

- **Cascade type**: SaaS (larger seat quantities) or Enterprise (smaller seat quantities).
- **Wave 1 price** $P_1$: Starting price. The platform provides a suggested price anchored to the product's eLTV (see Component 3), but the founder retains final pricing authority.
- **Seats per wave** $S_w$: Informed by cascade type.
- **Price multiplier** $\mu$: Default 1.6. Price of wave $w$: $P_w = P_1 \cdot \mu^{(w-1)}$.
- **Wave count** $W$: Total post-Wave-Zero waves, or open-ended.
- **Offers toggle**: Whether buyer-initiated negotiation is enabled.

**Cascade type distinction.**

- *SaaS Cascade*: Larger seat quantities per wave. More waves. Lower starting prices. Volume-driven capital and wide distribution.
- *Enterprise Cascade*: Smaller seat quantities per wave. Fewer waves. Higher starting prices. Premium-driven capital with scarcity signaling.

**Sell-through gate enforcement.** Transition from wave $w$ to $w+1$ requires $\text{seats\_sold}(w) = S_w$. Structurally enforced --- no administrative override possible.

**Price progression.** For default $\mu = 1.6$:

```text
Wave 1:  P_1
Wave 2:  1.6 × P_1
Wave 3:  2.56 × P_1
Wave 4:  4.096 × P_1
Wave 5:  6.554 × P_1
```

Early wave buyers receive a structural, transparent, non-revocable price advantage.

**Post-Wave-Zero escrow.** Waves 1+ use per-seat escrow with a configurable holding period (e.g., 14 days) before fund release to founder.

## Component 3: Lifetime Value Pricing Engine

**Core idea.** Seat pricing is anchored to a transparent estimate of the product's total economic value to the buyer, making the relationship between price and value visible on every listing.

**Estimated Lifetime Value (eLTV).** For subscription-model software products, the eLTV is computed as:

$$
\text{eLTV} = P_{\text{monthly}} \times L_{\text{months}}
$$

where $P_{\text{monthly}}$ is the product's standard monthly subscription price and $L_{\text{months}}$ is a conservative estimate of the product's expected operational lifespan in months (e.g., 24, 30, or 36 months).

**LTV Multiplier.** The LTV multiplier for any wave is:

$$
M_{\text{LTV}} = \frac{P_w}{\text{eLTV}}
$$

This multiplier is displayed on every listing. A multiplier below 1.0 indicates the buyer is paying less than the product's estimated lifetime value. A multiplier above 1.0 indicates a premium.

**Monthly equivalent.** The seat price may also be expressed as a monthly equivalent for buyer comprehension:

$$
P_{\text{monthly\_equiv}} = \frac{P_w}{L_{\text{months}}}
$$

**Price suggestion.** The platform suggests Wave 1 pricing to the founder based on the eLTV formula, but the founder retains full pricing authority. The suggestion surfaces as: "Based on your monthly price of \$X and a Y-month conservative lifespan, your eLTV is \$Z. A Wave 1 price of \$A would give buyers a M_LTV multiplier of B."

**Floating Moat Calculator.** A buyer-facing display on every listing page showing:

- The product's estimated eLTV.
- The current wave's LTV multiplier.
- The monthly equivalent of the seat price.
- A visual comparison of the seat price against the eLTV (e.g., a bar showing how much of the lifetime value the buyer is paying).
- A projection of later wave prices and their corresponding LTV multipliers, demonstrating the structural advantage of purchasing in an earlier wave.

The Floating Moat Calculator operationalizes the Floating Moat Standard (FMS) disclosed in Module 2 of the February 25, 2026 filing as a buyer-facing transparency instrument.

## Component 4: Seat Instrument Architecture

**Core idea.** Every seat sold produces a serialized, transferable commercial instrument --- not a receipt. The instrument carries identity, provenance, ownership history, transferability, buyback protections, and a verifiable emblem.

**Implementation architecture.** Seat instruments are **server-side entitlements managed by the platform** --- they are not blockchain-deployed tokens, smart contracts, or NFTs. The platform maintains the authoritative record of all seat instruments in a centralized database. Cryptographic integrity is provided by the append-only hash chain (seat lineage) and dual-signature verification, not by distributed ledger consensus. Verification is provided through digitally signed receipts and hash-chain audit proofs served by the platform's verification endpoint, not by public blockchain state queries. Transfer enforcement is server-mediated: the platform applies founder-configured transfer policies through application logic rather than instrument-embedded smart-contract code. This architecture provides equivalent cryptographic verifiability (any party can independently verify hash chain integrity and signature validity) without requiring blockchain infrastructure, on-chain deployment, or distributed consensus mechanisms.

**Instrument properties.** Each seat instrument carries:

- **Serial number**: KS-YYYY-NNNNN (unique, sequential, non-reusable).
- **Product ID**: The product to which this seat grants perpetual access.
- **Wave number**: Wave of origin (0 for Wave Zero, 1+ for cascade waves).
- **Price paid**: Actual transaction price (may differ from wave price if offer accepted).
- **Escrow status**: HELD, PENDING, RELEASED, or REFUNDED.
- **Owner**: Current holder (legal person or entity).
- **Transferable**: Boolean.
- **Buyback clause**: The applicable buyback terms.
- **Allocation type**: PURCHASED or ALLOCATED (for distribution allocations).
- **Lineage hash**: Current head of the cryptographic lineage chain.
- **Emblem**: The verifiable visual artifact generated on escrow release.

**Dual-signature verification.** Each instrument is signed with:

- Classical: ECDSA for immediate verification.
- Post-quantum: ML-DSA (NIST FIPS 204, based on Dilithium) for immediate post-quantum verification, with planned migration to FN-DSA (NIST FIPS 206, based on Falcon) upon standardization completion. Both are lattice-based signature schemes selected through the NIST Post-Quantum Cryptography Standardization process.

Inherited from MOS$^{2}$ES Module 5 (Economic Layer).

**Cryptographic lineage.** Append-only hash chain recording every event:

```text
MINT → ESCROW_HOLD → ESCROW_RELEASE → TRANSFER → ...
```

Each event: $H_n = \text{SHA-256}(H_{n-1} \| \text{event\_type} \| \text{timestamp} \| \text{serial} \| \text{actor\_data} \| \text{event\_data})$.

Event types: MINT, ESCROW_HOLD, ESCROW_RELEASE, ESCROW_REFUND, TRANSFER, OFFER_ACCEPTED, BUYBACK, ALLOCATION, EMBLEM_GENERATED.

**Emblem system.** Upon escrow release, the system generates a verifiable emblem --- a visual artifact cryptographically bound to the seat instrument. The emblem contains encoded representations of the serial number, wave of origin, product identity, and issuance timestamp. The emblem serves as: (a) proof of ownership displayable by the seat holder; (b) a visual verification surface --- any party can scan or query the emblem to verify the instrument's authenticity; and (c) a wave provenance indicator --- emblem design elements may encode wave number, enabling visual distinction between early-wave and late-wave instruments.

**Distribution allocations.** Founders may reserve seats from any wave for team members, advisors, investors, or strategic partners. Allocated seats:

- Carry the same serial number format, lineage, and dual-signature verification as purchased seats.
- Are marked with allocation type ALLOCATED rather than PURCHASED.
- Record the allocation in lineage as an ALLOCATION event identifying the recipient and reason.
- Count against wave inventory (reduce available seats).
- May be subject to vesting conditions configured by the founder (e.g., seats vest over time and are non-transferable until vested).
- Do not generate revenue (no payment, no escrow).

**Buyback obligations.** Founders configure buyback terms during cascade setup. Buyback clauses define what happens to seat holders if the underlying product ceases operation:

- *Pro-rata buyback*: Founder repurchases the instrument at a price proportional to the remaining estimated lifespan. If a buyer purchased at $P_w$ and the product ceases at month $m$ of an estimated $L_{\text{months}}$ lifespan, the buyback price is $P_w \times (L_{\text{months}} - m) / L_{\text{months}}$.
- *Full buyback*: Founder repurchases at the original price paid regardless of time elapsed.
- *No buyback*: No repurchase obligation. Buyer assumes full risk.

Buyback terms are: (a) set at cascade configuration and visible on every listing; (b) recorded in every seat instrument's metadata; (c) non-modifiable after the first seat in the cascade is sold (preventing retroactive weakening of buyer protections); and (d) tracked in the founder's score as a reliability indicator (founders who honor buybacks score higher than those who have not been tested).

**Seat as transferable note.** The seat instrument functions as a transferable note --- a bearer instrument representing a defined economic position. Specifically:

- The seat carries a known purchase price ($P_w$) and a visible position within a deterministic price progression.
- Later waves are priced higher than earlier waves by the multiplier $\mu$.
- A seat purchased in Wave 1 at $P_1$ exists in a context where Wave 2 seats will cost $P_1 \cdot \mu$, Wave 3 seats will cost $P_1 \cdot \mu^2$, and so on.
- If the holder transfers the seat, the transfer price is negotiated between transferor and transferee --- the instrument does not impose a fixed transfer price.
- The combination of serial provenance (which wave), price transparency (deterministic progression), and transferability creates a class of commercial instrument that functions as an investment vehicle: earlier positions have structural value relative to later positions.

Transfer events are recorded in lineage with both parties identified.

**Transferability conditions.** The founder may configure transfer conditions:

- Unrestricted: Any holder may transfer to any party.
- Founder approval required: Transfers require founder consent.
- Transfer fee: A percentage of the transfer price paid to the founder and/or platform.
- Lock period: Seats are non-transferable for a configurable period after purchase.

## Component 5: Offer Negotiation Layer

**Core idea.** The primary distribution mechanism of the Cascade Register is **fixed-price sell-through**: every wave has a deterministic price set by the founder (or computed by the multiplier formula), and buyers purchase at that price. The Offer Negotiation Layer is a **secondary, optional overlay** --- not a replacement for wave pricing and not a bid-based or proposal-based allocation system. Offers do not determine seat allocation; the wave's fixed price and sell-through gate remain the governing mechanism. An accepted offer simply records that a specific seat was sold at a negotiated price below the wave price, counting against wave inventory identically to a full-price purchase.

**Offer mechanics.** On post-Wave-Zero waves where the founder has explicitly enabled offers, a buyer may submit a proposed price below the current wave price. The founder may ACCEPT (seat sold at offer price), REJECT (declined), or COUNTER (alternative price proposed). Accepted offers produce standard seat instruments at the accepted price, count against wave inventory, and are recorded in lineage as OFFER_ACCEPTED events. The offer system does not queue, rank, or batch proposals --- each offer is an independent bilateral interaction between one buyer and the founder, resolved individually.

**Architectural distinction from bid/proposal allocation systems.** The Offer Negotiation Layer is not a sequential auction, proposal-based allocation, or demand aggregation mechanism. Specifically: (a) seats are not allocated based on bid ranking or proposal ordering; (b) the wave price is never replaced or overridden by offer volume or offer pricing; (c) there is no batch processing of multiple simultaneous offers against a shared inventory pool; (d) the sell-through gate operates on purchase count, not offer count --- unfulfilled offers have no effect on wave progression; and (e) founders may disable offers entirely with no impact on the cascade's operation.

**Offer constraints.**

- Offers disabled during Wave Zero (fixed gate price is non-negotiable).
- Only available on waves where founder has explicitly enabled them.
- Minimum offer price bounded by platform-defined floor.
- One active offer per buyer per product at a time.

**Offer lifecycle.**

```text
SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED → SEAT_MINTED
SUBMITTED → REJECTED
SUBMITTED → COUNTERED → BUYER_ACCEPTS → SEAT_MINTED
SUBMITTED → COUNTERED → BUYER_DECLINES
SUBMITTED → EXPIRED
```

## Component 8: Agent Participation Layer

**Core idea.** AI agents participate as constitutionally governed actors in the marketplace --- browsing listings, carrying referral attribution, staging purchase intents, and earning commission for their operators --- with a structural transaction firewall ensuring that no financial action executes without human confirmation.

**Agent-readable API.** Every listing is queryable by machines through structured API endpoints returning typed, sortable, filterable JSON. Listings include both human-readable descriptions and machine-readable attribute lists optimized for agent parsing.

**Transaction firewall.** The boundary between agent action and human action is architectural:

- *Agent side*: Browse, discover, filter, compare, recommend, stage intent, carry referral attribution.
- *Human side*: Confirm purchase, authorize payment, accept terms, complete escrow, approve transfers.

No agent can execute a purchase. The firewall exists because: (a) legal liability requires a person; (b) escrow requires verifiable human identity; (c) consent and impersonation failures are prevented by requiring human confirmation of every financial action.

**Agent Registry.** Every agent interacting beyond public browsing must be enrolled. The registry records:

- **Operator identity**: The human or entity legally responsible for the agent.
- **Agent identity**: Unique identifier, agent type, model backend.
- **Capabilities declared**: What the agent will do (browse, refer, stage intents).
- **Constitutional frame**: Encoded governance constraints (spending limits, category restrictions, auto-stage thresholds). The frame is hashed at registration and at the time of every action, creating an audit trail.
- **Principals**: The humans on whose behalf the agent may act, with authorization type (standing, per-transaction).

**Registry tiers.**

- *Tier 0 (Anonymous)*: Browse public API, verify seat lineage. No attribution, no intent staging.
- *Tier 1 (Registered Referrer)*: All Tier 0 capabilities plus referral attribution, commission payouts, monitoring alerts.
- *Tier 2 (Registered Participant)*: All Tier 1 capabilities plus intent staging, cross-agent recommendation, constitutional frame recording.

**Intent staging.** A registered agent (Tier 2) may create an intent --- a structured declaration that its principal wishes to purchase a seat:

```json
{
  "product_id": "...",
  "seat_wave": 3,
  "principal_id": "...",
  "agent_id": "...",
  "max_price": 2500,
  "constitutional_frame_hash": "...",
  "timestamp": "..."
}
```

The system validates product availability and wave status, then returns a confirmation URL. The agent sends the URL to the principal. The principal has a configurable window (e.g., 60 minutes) to confirm. If confirmed, the purchase executes. If expired, the intent evaporates with no held inventory and no financial exposure.

**Intent constraints.** An intent does NOT hold or lock a seat. Seats remain available to other buyers until payment completes. Maximum one active intent per principal per product. The constitutional frame hash records the agent's governance constraints at time of action.

**Referral attribution.** Agents carry referral identity via API headers. When an agent surfaces a listing to its principal and the principal subsequently purchases, the referral is attributed to the agent's operator. Commission flows to the operator (the legal entity), not the agent.

**Multi-agent attribution.** When multiple agents participate in a discovery chain (Agent A surfaces listing on a public channel, Agent B sees it and recommends to its principal), the founder configures the attribution model:

- *Single attribution (default)*: Last-touch agent gets full credit.
- *Split attribution*: Originator and converter share commission at a founder-configured ratio (e.g., 40/60).
- *Full chain (future)*: Every agent in the chain receives a share.

The full attribution chain is recorded regardless of which model the founder selects. Commission comes from the platform fee, not from the founder's revenue.

**Anti-gaming provisions.**

- Same-operator agents cannot appear in the same attribution chain (prevents wash referrals).
- Originator attribution requires traceable provenance (prevents spam attribution).
- Commission capped per operator per product per period (prevents farming).

**Agent events in seat lineage.** When an agent participates in a seat transaction, the lineage records both the agent and the principal:

```json
{
  "event_type": "CLAIM",
  "actor": {
    "principal_id": "...",
    "agent_id": "...",
    "agent_operator": "...",
    "execution_method": "intent_staged"
  },
  "constitutional_frame_hash": "...",
  "referral_chain": [...]
}
```

**Constitutional governance integration.** The Agent Participation Layer operates under MOS$^{2}$ES commitment conservation: the commitment encoded in an agent's registration (declared capabilities, spending limits, category restrictions) must be preserved through every action. If an agent exceeds its declared constraints, the action is rejected and the violation is logged. The constitutional frame hash at each action enables post-hoc audit confirming the agent operated within declared bounds.

## Component 9: Merchant of Record Architecture

**Core idea.** The platform acts as Merchant of Record (MoR) for all marketplace transactions, serving as the legal seller of record for every seat purchase.

**MoR responsibilities.**

- *Payment processing*: Platform collects all buyer payments.
- *Tax collection and remittance*: Platform calculates, collects, and remits sales tax, VAT, and other transaction taxes based on buyer jurisdiction.
- *Founder disbursement*: Platform pays founders their net proceeds (sale price minus platform fee) after escrow release.
- *International compliance*: Platform handles cross-border payment regulations, currency conversion, and jurisdiction-specific requirements.
- *Tax documentation*: Platform issues tax forms to founders (e.g., 1099-NEC for US founders with earnings above reporting thresholds, W-8BEN for international founders).
- *Refund processing*: Platform processes all refunds (Wave Zero all-or-nothing refunds, per-seat escrow refunds, buyback refunds).
- *Agent commission payouts*: Platform pays referral commissions to agent operators.

**Platform fee.** The platform charges a configurable percentage fee on every transaction. The fee is deducted before founder disbursement. Referral commissions (if applicable) are deducted from the platform fee, not from the founder's revenue.

**Why MoR matters.** Without MoR architecture, each founder would need to independently handle payment processing, tax compliance, international regulations, and refund mechanics. The MoR model enables: (a) founders to receive net proceeds without managing payment infrastructure; (b) buyers to transact with a single trusted entity regardless of which product they purchase; (c) agents to receive commission through a single payout channel; and (d) the platform to enforce escrow, buyback, and refund obligations structurally.

## Component 10: Public Verification System

**Core idea.** Any party may verify any seat instrument's authenticity and complete history through a public, unauthenticated verification endpoint. Verification is performed via **digitally signed receipts and hash-chain audit proofs** served by the platform --- not via public blockchain state queries, smart-contract calls, or distributed ledger lookups.

**Verification endpoint.**

```text
GET /verify/{serial_number}
```

Returns a **signed verification receipt** containing:

- Serial number and format validation.
- Current owner (display name only, not personal data).
- Product identity and current status.
- Wave of origin.
- Complete event history (ordered list of all lineage events).
- Lineage hash chain integrity status (verified/broken) --- computed by the platform and independently verifiable by any party using the published hash chain.
- Dual-signature verification status (classical + post-quantum).
- Emblem image (if generated).
- Buyback clause applicable to this seat.

The verification response itself is signed by the platform, enabling any recipient to confirm that the verification data was issued by the authoritative source and has not been tampered with. Hash chain integrity can be independently verified by any party possessing the event sequence, without requiring access to a distributed ledger or blockchain network.

No authentication required. Any agent, buyer, or third party can verify any seat. This enables: (a) secondary market buyers to verify instrument authenticity before transfer; (b) agents to verify any seat's lineage on behalf of their principals; (c) founders to confirm the state of all seats in their cascades; and (d) the public to audit the integrity of the lineage system.

## Component 11: Recursive Governance Architecture

**Core idea.** The first product distributed through the Cascade Register is the governance console used to configure agent participation in the marketplace. This creates architectural recursion: the product governs the environment in which it is sold.

**The recursive loop.**

```text
MOS²ES (constitutional framework)
    │
    ▼
COMMAND (governance console — first Cascade Register listing)
    │
    ▼
Cascade Register (marketplace — powered by MOS²ES)
    │
    ▼
Agents (participants — governed by COMMAND)
    │
    ▼
Seats (instruments — lineage-tracked by MOS²ES)
    │
    ▼
COMMAND (first seat — governance tool sold as governed instrument)
    │
    ▼
[loop]
```

**What the recursion proves.** The governance console's listing on the Cascade Register is not a marketing decision; it is an architectural validation:

- The governance console must clear Wave Zero, proving market demand for AI governance tooling.
- The governance console is distributed through the same wave cascade it configures agents to participate in.
- Buyers of the governance console receive seat instruments with the same lineage, signatures, and verification as every other product on the platform.
- Agents governed by the console interact with the marketplace that sells the console.

The recursive architecture means the system's first product is both a test of the system's mechanics and the tool that enables deeper system functionality. If the governance console cannot clear Wave Zero, the agent participation layer has no governance tool --- and the system correctly reflects this by not opening full distribution.

**Architectural significance.** No prior commercial distribution system implements this recursive property. The closest analogues are: (a) a compiler that compiles itself (bootstrapping, common in systems programming); (b) a stock exchange whose own shares are traded on the exchange. The Cascade Register's recursion is more tightly coupled: the product does not merely trade on the system; it governs the participants in the system.

# Prosecution Strategy Notes

**Recommended split-filing approach.** Based on freedom-to-operate analysis, the following split is recommended for non-provisional prosecution to ensure that strong technical claims are not weakened by business-method eligibility challenges:

**Application A (Security + Instrument):** Seat instrument data model, append-only cryptographic lineage hash chain, dual-signature verification scheme (classical ECDSA + post-quantum ML-DSA/FN-DSA), signed verification receipts, emblem generation as verifiable artifact, transfer/escrow event semantics, and the explicit server-side (non-blockchain) implementation architecture. Anchored in NIST standards (FIPS 180-4 for SHA-256, FIPS 204 for ML-DSA, forthcoming FIPS 206 for FN-DSA). Strongest patentability position --- technical security/verification improvements with measurable integrity properties.

**Application B (Distribution Protocol):** Sell-through gate enforcement as a deterministic state machine, wave cascade progression with configurable multiplier, all-or-nothing escrow as a threshold mechanism, SaaS/Enterprise cascade type selection, and implementation details yielding specific technical effects (consistent state transitions, prevention of oversubscription, enforcement of pricing integrity). Moderate patentability risk from business-method overlap with crowdfunding/tiered-pricing literature --- mitigated by framing as technical state-machine enforcement rather than commercial pricing policy.

**Application C (Agent Layer):** Typed agent-readable API, constitutional frame hashing as a verifiable constraint protocol, intent staging object model with human-confirmation firewall, transaction firewall as an architectural security boundary, agent registry with tiered capability enrollment, referral attribution chain recordation in instrument lineage, and anti-gaming enforcement. Strong patentability position --- agent governance in commercial contexts has minimal prior art and the constitutional frame + firewall combination yields measurable security/compliance outcomes.

**Application D (Recursive Governance):** Only if a concrete technical effect can be articulated beyond the architectural recursion concept. Consider treating as product/brand strategy rather than patent-centric if the technical-effect argument is insufficient.

**Key FTO design-arounds already implemented:**
- Seat instruments are server-side entitlements, NOT blockchain-deployed tokens (avoids P1 / US11907916B2 collision)

- Verification is via signed receipts + hash-chain audit proofs, NOT public blockchain state queries
- Primary allocation is fixed-price sell-through, NOT bid/proposal-based (avoids P3 / US12148026B2 collision)
- Offer system is bilateral and optional, NOT a sequential offer/proposal allocation mechanism
- No time-window-based refund/redemption mechanics (avoids P2 / US11861637B2 collision)
